Meet the Founder

The Woman Behind the Green Nectar

Biotechnology Engineer. Foodtech entrepreneur. Farmer's granddaughter. And the mother who refused to accept that "healthy" and "delicious" had to be in conflict.

⚡ The Moment That Started It All
"He had eaten two packets. Two — of a snack that turned out to be mostly refined flour, artificially coloured to look like carrot."

It started with a rushed school pickup, a cheerful packet of "Veggie Puffs," and a child happily crunching away in the back seat. The packaging declared — in bold green letters — "Made with real vegetables!" It looked wholesome. It smelled savoury. A child who simply wanted something crunchy had no reason to question it.

That evening, Shravani turned the packet over and read the ingredients list the way only a Biotechnology Engineer reads one — slowly, and with growing alarm. The word "carrot" appeared ninth on a fourteen-ingredient list, wedged between two additives she recognised from her university toxicology modules.

The snack wasn't dangerous. But the realisation was: the entire category of children's food had quietly decided that kids couldn't handle real taste. That night, she wrote one question at the top of a blank page: "What would a snack look like if it started with the vegetable, not the chemistry?"

From a Grandmother's Kitchen to a Product Range

Shravani grew up in Telangana in a joint family where her grandfather farmed jowar and her grandmother made everything from scratch. Her training in Biotechnology gave her the science to understand why that food was good. When those two worlds met in a kitchen — with two children as the world's most honest taste-testers — Green Nectar Essentials began to take shape.

Built Over Time, One Recipe at a Time

What began as weekend kitchen experiments evolved slowly and stubbornly into a proper product range. Early versions were rejected by the most demanding critics in the house. Textures were wrong. Flavours were "too healthy." Colours were "weird." Every failure was a brief. Over time, through visits to local millet farms and conversations with cooperative farmers, the recipes found their balance — nutritious enough to matter, delicious enough that children chose them.

Green Nectar Essentials launched in Hyderabad with a single belief: children deserve food that is genuinely good — not just food that is marketed as good.

From Honest Food to Honest Packaging: SipWise

The same question that started Green Nectar — what is really in this? — soon turned to the bottle itself. Every day, millions of plastic water and milk bottles are used once and thrown away. SipWise is Green Nectar's answer: drinking water and fresh milk in plant-based bottles that are certified compostable, with a take-back service that makes sure they are composted rather than dumped.

"A child who eats a beetroot gummy and knows it's beetroot is a child learning to love real food. That is the entire point."
